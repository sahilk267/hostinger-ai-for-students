# Research notes: making AI for Students more valuable

## Source 1 — Adaptive learning review
URL: https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7102/13/12/1216

The review argues that adaptive learning is strongest when it personalizes content and pace, provides real-time feedback and progress tracking, and recommends relevant materials. It also flags privacy, algorithmic bias and the continuing need for effective teacher–student interaction as important constraints. Product implication: AI for Students should measure demonstrated skills and adapt the next task, rather than only expose a large catalog.

## Source 2 — Duolingo Max product announcement
URL: https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-max/

Duolingo’s AI features are framed as interactive practice: real-time conversation, roleplay scenarios, post-interaction feedback, transcripts for review and human-authored scenarios. Product implication: a strong AI learning loop is scenario → learner action → feedback → review, with content boundaries and human review. A certificate or streak without a demonstrated artifact is weaker than a visible, reviewed performance.

## Source 3 — Washington University Center for Teaching and Learning
URL: https://ctl.wustl.edu/resources/using-retrieval-practice-to-increase-student-learning/

The resource summarizes evidence that retrieval practice generally outperforms repeated studying for longer-term retention and can support higher-order thinking and metacognition. It recommends low-stakes recall, brain dumps and teach-back activities, with timely feedback; it also emphasizes desirable difficulty and engaging every learner. Product implication: each daily mission should require the learner to produce something from memory or apply a concept, then receive feedback—not merely read a lesson or tick a box.

## Source 4 — Self-Determination Theory overview
URL: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/theory/

The framework identifies autonomy, competence and relatedness as conditions that support high-quality motivation, persistence and creativity. Product implication: let learners choose relevant mission tracks, show concrete mastery evidence and optionally share with peers; do not rely only on coercive streaks, public rankings or shame for retention.

## Source 5 — UNESCO guidance for generative AI in education and research
URL: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research

UNESCO’s guidance emphasizes privacy protection, age-appropriate access, human agency and pedagogical design when GenAI is used in education. Product implication: the platform needs clear data boundaries, age-appropriate language, an explicit “AI assists; learner decides” posture and reviewable outputs.

## Source 6 — Stanford Teaching Commons AI literacy framework
URL: https://teachingcommons.stanford.edu/teaching-guides/artificial-intelligence-teaching-guide/understanding-ai-literacy

Stanford organizes AI literacy into four intersecting domains: functional literacy (how AI works), ethical literacy (bias, privacy, integrity and equity), rhetorical literacy (using and critiquing language) and pedagogical literacy (using AI to enhance learning). It describes progression from novice awareness to intermediate analysis/application and advanced creation. Product implication: AI for Students should issue evidence-based skill stamps across these four domains instead of treating prompting as the whole subject.

## Source 7 — Digital Promise project-based learning research map
URL: https://digitalpromise.org/research-map/topics/project-based-learning/

Digital Promise describes project-based learning as an authentic problem or question, investigation/design activity, final product and often collaboration. Its synthesis reports potential gains in achievement, transfer, engagement, problem solving, autonomy and collaboration, while warning that projects need guidance, aligned goals and reflection. Product implication: the strongest shareable milestone is a small artifact tied to a clear skill, with reflection and feedback—not a decorative badge.

## Source 8 — University of Iowa learning portfolios
URL: https://assessment.uiowa.edu/using-student-learning-portfolios-departmental-outcomes-assessment

The resource describes portfolios as documentation and reflection over time: learners select representative work, explain why they selected it, respond to feedback and identify next steps. Product implication: the 30-day Journey should accumulate a selective “AI proof portfolio” with artifact, skill demonstrated, learner reflection and optional share controls. Sharing should be user-controlled and avoid exposing private prompts, assignments or personal data.

## Source 9 — Khanmigo official product page
URL: https://www.khanmigo.ai/

Khanmigo positions itself as a tutor that challenges learners to think critically, guides them toward answers rather than directly answering, and supports subjects including writing and coding. Product implication: AI for Students should differentiate from ChatGPT by asking a useful follow-up, requiring learner work, and giving targeted feedback or hints rather than generating a finished answer by default.

## Source 10 — Google Learn About experiment
URL: https://learning.google.com/experiments/learn-about/signup

Google describes a conversational learning companion that accepts questions or uploaded material, offers interactive guides and learning aids, helps learners simplify or go deeper, and connects them to relevant images, videos and articles. Product implication: a fixed curriculum should still allow curiosity branches: “go deeper,” “show a simpler explanation,” source trail and optional upload/input paths, while retaining the 30-day skill arc.
